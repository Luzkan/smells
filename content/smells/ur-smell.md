## Primordial Smell

*Also known as: Ur-smell, Original-Sin Smell*

**Definition:** The developer no longer looks outside themselves for knowledge, review or correction. This stems from narcissistic traits formed when a child did not receive enough *mirroring*, in Kohut's sense: a caregiver's delight that reflects back the child's worth. The unmet need survives into adulthood in its *archaic* form, as a self that can't afford to be wrong. The coder treats their own judgment as the final authority and in effect mistakes themselves for God. From this, many other smells follow.

### Symptoms

- "I don't need to read the books."
- No one reviews their code, and they have no peer they run ideas by.
- Hand-rolled solutions to problems that were solved long ago.
- Language idioms and published standards are ignored.
- Correction is felt as an attack rather than as information.

### Derived Smells

Speculative Generality, Long Method, God Class, Inappropriate Intimacy, Comments that explain nothing, and the absence of tests.

### Treatment

Bring in outside sources of truth: citations to the canon, code review, and people whose loyalty is to the truth rather than to the developer. The fix takes ongoing effort, like exercise. It is the good kind of tiring, not the masking kind.

### Caveat

Not every smell is primordial. Many come from deadlines, bad incentives, inherited codebases or plain inexperience, written by people whose childhoods were fine.

### References

- Kohut, H. (1971). *The Analysis of the Self.* International Universities Press.
- Kohut, H. (1977). *The Restoration of the Self.* International Universities Press.
- Fowler, M. (2018). *Refactoring: Improving the Design of Existing Code* (2nd ed.), ch. 3. Addison-Wesley.

> **In one sentence:** a developer's refusal to look outside themselves for knowledge or correction, rooted in archaic narcissistic needs left by a childhood lack of mirroring (Kohut), from which many other code smells derive.
